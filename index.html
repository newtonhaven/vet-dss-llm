VetDSS: Veterinary Decision Support with a Local LLM

Colab Code Github Code Datasets

VetDSS suggests possible illnesses for a cat or dog from a checklist of symptoms, and lists the additional symptoms a veterinarian should check to confirm or rule out each one.

Research and learning project, not a medical device. Outputs can be wrong or incomplete and must not replace an examination by a licensed veterinarian.

Example

Abstract

Veterinarians work under time pressure, and symptoms in animals often overlap between conditions. VetDSS is an assistant that correlates reported symptoms with possible illnesses, points out symptoms that may have been overlooked, and suggests what to check next, instead of giving one confident diagnosis.

The user picks the species (dog or cat), enters an age and ticks symptoms from a fixed list of 26. The case is matched against a small veterinary knowledge base, and Mistral-7B-Instruct v0.3, loaded in 4-bit, lists candidate illnesses together with the additional symptoms that would confirm each one, and recommends consulting a veterinarian.

VetDSS is a research and learning project. Its results come from a handful of hand-checked cases rather than a benchmark, and it is not a medical device.



How it works

  1. Structured input. Species, age and symptoms from a fixed list of 26. Limiting input to known terms keeps queries close to the vocabulary of the knowledge base and reduces off-topic prompts.
  2. Retrieval. Every passage in the knowledge base is embedded once with sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and stored in a FAISS IndexFlatL2 index. At query time, the five closest passages are retrieved.
  3. Generation. The retrieved passages, the case details and a fixed instruction set are sent to Mistral-7B-Instruct v0.3 through its chat template.
  4. Output. Candidate illnesses, the extra symptoms that would confirm each, and a recommendation to consult a veterinarian.

The model is instructed to be clear, concise and actionable; to list several fitting illnesses with the symptoms needed to confirm each; to avoid speculation and alarming language; to put the animal's wellbeing first; and to say so rather than invent an answer when it does not know.



Model and results

Mistral-7B-Instruct v0.3 (Apache 2.0) is loaded with 4-bit NF4 quantization through bitsandbytes, which cuts its memory footprint from 14.50 GB (fp16) to 4.03 GB, a 72% reduction. Decoding uses sampling at temperature 0.7 with up to 250 new tokens, so the same input can give different answers.

Early experiments were qualitative checks on single cases, not a benchmark. For a feline upper respiratory infection (URI) case, the model without reference material listed URI second; with retrieved passages it named URI as the most likely cause, with rhinitis and feline herpesvirus as alternatives. On a long, mixed symptom list, cleaning the data, adding retrieved context and tightening the instructions produced answers that kept conditions separate and listed the missing symptoms for each.

Dataset

The knowledge base is built from the VetPetCare and Animal Disease datasets and ASPCA articles. It was cleaned (formats normalized, special characters fixed, unclear entries corrected) and is stored as structured JSON and CSV in the repository's Data/ folder. See the Datasets page for details.



Limitations

  • No quantitative evaluation yet. Results come from a handful of hand-checked cases.
  • Small, uneven knowledge base. There is more dog material than cat material, and coverage of rare conditions is thin.
  • Fixed symptom list. Only 26 symptoms can be selected, so conditions with other signs cannot be described.
  • Generic embeddings. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is not trained on veterinary text, so related terms can be missed.
  • Sampling output. With temperature 0.7, the same input can give different answers.
